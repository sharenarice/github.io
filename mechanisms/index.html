Introduction

A Structural Canon for Builders, Evaluators, and Institutions

5 chapters 15 primary mechanisms 40 sub-mechanisms 55 named patterns CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

Medical neurotechnology asks teams to turn insight into interventions that are scientifically sound, clinically meaningful, reliable in practice, and durable under real-world constraints. In this field, progress is shaped more by structure than by talent or effort. MECHANISMS names the structures: fifty-five patterns that reliably produce similar outcomes under similar institutional conditions, across training, funding, translation, quality, and programs.

Diagnostic by Design

This canon names causes. It does not prescribe fixes, tell case studies, or assign blame. The patterns are structural: they persist regardless of how talented or motivated the people inside them are. Naming them precisely is the prerequisite for changing them.

What a Mechanism Is

A mechanism is a pattern that reliably produces similar outcomes under similar institutional conditions. Mechanisms are conditional: they activate under specific system states. They are bidirectional: each failure implies a working version. They are actionable: each names where structure would have to change.

Both Directions at Once

Every pattern in this canon has a positive counterpart: what the working version looks like. Each mechanism card below carries a toggle that flips between the failure and the structure worth building. The canon reads as a warning list or a design guide, depending on which side you hold.

Orientation

Five Chapters, Upstream to Downstream

The chapters run in causal order. Training systems shape the people. Capital shapes the accountability those people inherit. Translation shapes whether knowledge moves. Quality shapes whether learning compounds. Programs shape the institutional layer that funds and evaluates all of it. Later failures usually begin earlier than they surface.

1

How Training Systems Shape Authority, Labor, and Judgment

Most downstream failures begin here, before a product exists: in the systems that shape who gets authority, who absorbs risk, and whose judgment is taken seriously.

Technical expertise accumulates through training systems that withhold decision authority until a formal transition point. The result is capability without governance exposure: people who understand the work cannot govern it. The gap is a structural feature, not a training deficit.

  • Preparation extends without exposing people to decisions that have real consequences
  • Long evaluation environments train people to guess what evaluators want rather than think independently
  • The people closest to the work are structurally excluded from making decisions
Working version · Authority Formation

Authority grows alongside expertise. People who understand the work deeply are trusted to make real decisions with significant consequences.

Financial, time-based, and identity costs accumulate faster than real alternatives materialize. Staying is not an endorsement. It is the rational response to a shrinking set of options. Dependency stalls authority formation through limited options rather than lack of ability.

  • Financial and benefits dependencies make leaving practically impossible
  • Undefined advancement criteria keep people waiting indefinitely
  • Mission and identity framing substitutes for fair compensation and keeps people from leaving
Working version · Fair Dependency

Fair relationships between institutions and people. Individuals have access to the resources they need and keep a clear path to other opportunities.

Funding pressure, reputational cost, diffuse collaboration, and IP rules each reward continuity over correction. The institution optimizes for its own persistence. Effectiveness becomes optional; stability does not.

  • Individuals adapt their work to whatever gets funded rather than what the problem requires
  • Reputation and status make it too costly to disagree or change direction
  • Multi-institution collaboration spreads responsibility so broadly that no one can make a decisive call
  • University IP ownership and royalty rules create misaligned incentives between inventors and institutions
Working version · Honest Institutions

Institutions that stay honest over time. They maintain consistent standards and institutional memory while keeping the ability to change direction based on evidence. Value from intellectual property gets a fair distribution: the researchers who created the work hold a meaningful stake, and the institution supports deployment.

Knowledge optimized for internal audiences cannot be used by people outside the producing system. Visibility and brand-building substitute for the work of making knowledge usable. Valid results become unusable at the boundary.

  • Knowledge optimized for internal audiences cannot be used by people outside the producing system
  • Institutions replace the work of making knowledge usable with visibility and brand-building
  • Researchers and decision-makers optimize for different goals, making valid knowledge unusable
Working version · Knowledge Transfer

Knowledge that stays complete during transfer. Assumptions are documented, limits are identified, and the criteria for readiness are clear to everyone who uses it.

2

How Funding, Regulation, and Advice Shape What Gets Built

Capable teams often fail not because of their skills, but because the structures around them reward deferral over resolution.

The source of capital determines which risks an organization is required to resolve and which it is permitted to defer. This configuration happens at investment, before any work begins.

  • Venture capital tracks fundability more than product readiness
  • Capital without formal accountability allows organizations to drift indefinitely
  • Public and nonprofit funding defines success by participation and compliance rather than delivery
Working version · Capital Accountability

Funding structures that require organizations to resolve hard questions instead of demonstrating that activity is occurring.

Internal checks deferred under time pressure accumulate as a design-level problem that surfaces at the worst possible moment. Accountability fails before resources run out.

  • Organizations get stuck in pilot mode because no one can end the test
  • Organizations treat available funding as a substitute for regulatory readiness
  • Unresolved organizational problems get offloaded onto a small number of people who absorb them silently
Working version · Resolution

Decisions made in logical order. Pilots and tests answer specific questions rather than keeping every option open indefinitely.

Advisors influence decisions without sharing the costs when those decisions fail. Credentials and status outrank the knowledge of people doing the work. When responsibility is spread out, people stop exercising independent judgment and defer.

  • Advisors influence decisions without sharing the costs when those decisions fail
  • Organizations trust credentials and status over the knowledge of people doing the work
  • When responsibility is spread out, people stop exercising independent judgment and simply defer
Working version · Advisory Alignment

Advisors who share the consequences when their guidance fails. Shared risk keeps advice practical and useful.

Product language expands one reasonable-sounding word at a time. Each step looks small. The accumulation tells a story the evidence cannot deliver, and correction gets more expensive the longer it waits.

  • Commercial pressure causes product language to slowly expand past what the evidence supports
  • Organizations reframe medical products as wellness tools to avoid regulatory classification
  • Organizations construct regulatory justifications after claims are already publicly made
Working version · Claims Discipline

Product claims strictly based on current evidence. This builds long-term credibility instead of future risk.

3

How Knowledge Moves, Stalls, and Gets Distorted

Research, engineering, and clinical work each generate valid evidence inside their own systems. Without enforced connections, that evidence stays trapped at its origin.

Clinical, research, and engineering teams each optimize for their own domain. Timelines, incentives, and standards of evidence diverge. Each team does excellent work. The work does not connect.

  • Clinical, research, and engineering teams each optimize for their own domain and cannot produce usable results together
  • Evidence generated after key decisions are already made cannot change those decisions
  • Animal and human evidence programs run independently and produce results that cannot be combined
Working version · Translation Coupling

Research, clinical practice, and engineering connected through shared accountability and synchronized timelines. Each area informs the work of the others.

Weak early results become strategic commitments through storytelling before the evidence warrants it. Measurement choices lock in. The story hardens before the evidence does, and walking it back costs more than never telling it.

  • Weak early results become strategic commitments through storytelling before the evidence warrants it
  • Early measurement choices become locked in and prevent learning from moving forward
Working version · Evidence Discipline

Evidence communicated with complete honesty. It specifies what was tested, what remains unknown, and the conditions required for findings to hold.

4

Quality Makes or Breaks Devices

Quality is one of the few structural investments that compresses the effective timeline of medical device development. It is the condition that makes the process possible, not overhead layered on top of it.

Quality determines whether a device built under one team can be understood, defended, and operated by another. Absent or misplaced, every transition becomes a restart, and every audit becomes a reconstruction.

  • Quality cannot be inferred from good intentions, delegated to consultants, or rebuilt after the fact
  • Quality authority placed too far from the work cannot govern decisions when it matters
  • Skipped quality steps accumulate into costs that eventually exceed what the organization can afford to fix
  • Open-source components without clear ownership dissolve the accountability chains quality requires
Working version · Quality Infrastructure

Quality systems that preserve decisions over time. Personnel changes become smooth transitions, and audits build on a complete, clear record.

Regulatory clearance is one gate among several. Hospitals and payers require evidence the clearance file was never designed to hold. A device prepared only for the regulator stalls at the next gate.

Working version · Quality for Deployment

Quality infrastructure designed for the entire product lifecycle. It supports everything from initial clearance through clinical use and hospital payment.

5

How Programs Shape What Gets Funded, Learned, and Built

A program is not a neutral space for work. It is a set of decisions about what kinds of people, problems, and answers will receive rewards.

Selection criteria, evaluation practices, and permitted risk are set at the design stage. Those incentives dominate what happens regardless of what the program intends.

  • Who gets selected into a program shapes what problems get worked on before any work begins
  • Program affiliation substitutes for evidence of actual progress in how participants are evaluated
  • Program structures suppress bold risk-taking by penalizing any deviation from expected paths
Working version · Program Incentives

Programs designed to solve the specific problems they were funded to address. Selection rewards the question, evaluation rewards resolution, and principled risk stays survivable.

Milestones signal progress without guaranteeing that real questions were answered. Fixed timelines end before uncertainty resolves, and unfinished problems pass to the next group with no context for where they came from.

  • Milestones signal progress without guaranteeing that real questions were answered
  • Programs spread resources too thin and lack the authority to stop work that is not producing learning
  • Fixed program timelines end before uncertainty is resolved and pass unfinished problems to the next group
Working version · Matched Design

Time, resources, and learning targets sized to the uncertainty the program was built to resolve. Work ends when questions close, not when calendars do.

Positive Mechanism

This mechanism names the working configuration directly: a well-defined problem, real authority to stop unproductive work, and consequences tied to learning. Programs with these conditions produce actual answers and work the next group can build on. The conditions are rare because each one requires giving up a form of institutional comfort.

Working version

This mechanism is the working version. When the three conditions are present together, the program resolves the questions it was designed to resolve.

Co-occurrence

Failures Travel in Stacks

These patterns rarely occur alone. Four combinations appear together often enough to deserve their own names. Recognizing the stack tells you where to act, because the counter-move for a stack differs from the counter-move for any single member.

1.12.12.3

The Early-Stage Authority-Accountability Stack

Delayed authority, capital that dictates accountability, and advisors who fill the leadership gap. Common in new organizations where governance is still forming. The result is high activity that resolves no core uncertainty.

Counter-move: establish formal decision-making power before the next funding round.

1.43.13.2

The Translation Failure Stack

Knowledge transfer fails, domains stay disconnected, and early signals get overcommitted. Frequent in university spinouts: knowledge built for academics, promises made before translation work has happened.

Counter-move: build translation infrastructure before findings go public.

2.24.14.2

The Quality Debt Stack

Deferred decisions, absent quality systems, and failure at the reimbursement stage. Clearance arrives with minimal effort; hospitals and payers then ask for documentation nobody prepared.

Counter-move: build quality to provider and payer standards from the start of development.

1.35.15.2

The Program Recycling Stack

Institutional incentive locks, programs with wrong incentives, and mismatched timing. Common in government and foundation initiatives that reward participation to stay legitimate. Unanswered questions pass from one program to the next.

Counter-move: define success as the resolution of specific questions before the program design is final.

Field Use

Name the Pattern, Not the Person

The canon is built to be spoken, not only read. Mechanisms give teams a shared vocabulary for problems that would otherwise be discussed as personalities, culture, or bad luck. A pattern named neutrally can be worked on. A person blamed cannot.

For Builders

Use mechanism names to raise structural concerns without accusation. A one-page memo comparing two documents does more than a hallway complaint. The pattern is the subject; no one has to be the villain.

For Evaluators

Use the signals to distinguish real progress from performative progress. Milestones met, papers published, and rounds closed are activity measures. The evaluative question is whether the underlying uncertainty closed.

For Institutions

Use the positive counterparts as design requirements. Each working version describes a structure that can be specified, funded, and audited: authority that grows with expertise, capital that requires resolution, quality that preserves decisions.

When a credentialed organization is not producing judgment at the level its people are capable of, the deeper question is not a broken culture. It is where decision authority sits relative to the people with the most contextual knowledge, and what transition conditions keep it there.
