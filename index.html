What I do

Clinical Neuro · Neurotechnology · Neuroscience

I work on making neuro work.

Serving as Head of Neuroscience Clinical Trials and Partnerships at Radial, the largest brain medicine clinical network in the United States.

My experience has been hands on and direct, which matters a great deal for closing gaps: early clinical work, basic research, device testing and characterization, tooling, documentation, training teams on protocols, diligence in venture capital, journalism, and advising organizations.

My core strength is structural synthesis. I have produced field-level analyses and diligence frameworks. I publish many reference materials that transfer information rather than hype. I am especially interested in capital-accountability coupling: what makes neuro initiatives investable, auditable, and resilient.

My flagship work, Field Potentials, is a systems account of clinical neuro: the seats people hold, the gates an intervention must pass, and the twenty-two mechanisms that decide whether good work reaches patients. It is free.

Field Potentials

A Systems Account of Clinical Neuro

Clinical neuro keeps its promises more often when the people in it can see past their own seat. Most of the field’s failures happen at the seams, where a researcher, a regulator, a payer, a clinician, and a builder each hold one piece and no one is paid to watch the joins.

Field Potentials maps those seams. It is written for anyone who works in clinical neuro, drugs and devices alike, or lives with what it produces, and it assumes little beyond curiosity.

  • 12seats, from trainee and discovery researcher to payer and person with lived experience, with who pays for each
  • 15words that mean different things in different seats, set side by side in the Neurosetta Stone
  • 7gates an intervention passes to reach a patient and stay in use
  • 22field diagnostic mechanisms, each with a key question to ask aloud and a first move from your seat

Field Potentials supersedes MECHANISMS, my earlier account of medical neurotechnology.

About

Building at the
intersection of fields

I earned a PhD in neuroscience from the University of Michigan. My discovery research focused on brain rhythms and neural circuits. Today I work across clinical trials, product, writing, and strategy in neurotechnology.

My work turns hard-won knowledge into tools and resources others can use.

A few notes on how I got here: as a child, I wrote a 45-page typed documentary on human behavior just because I was curious. My dream was to be a scientist and write books about my inventions. I also lived and trained at the Ann Arbor Zen Buddhist Temple for 22 months during graduate school. You cannot meditate your way through the FDA, but you can learn to observe lines of thought.
